### Draft message

**Subject:** Food-establishment classification question — Halifax household buying club

Hello,

I am developing a Halifax-based household buying club called HarbourCart and would like written guidance on the food-establishment classification and operating requirements for the specific phase-1 workflow below.

Customers would place non-binding demand signals online first. For a real pilot, customers would pre-order before procurement. HarbourCart would purchase only:

1. fresh, whole, unprocessed fruit and vegetables; and
2. non-potentially-hazardous grocery products that remain in their original manufacturer-sealed consumer packages.

The phase-1 operation would not cook, cut, peel, wash, prepare, decant, or repackage meat, dairy, seafood, ready-to-eat food, or temperature-controlled food.

Once weekly, suppliers would deliver to a Halifax-area pickup/fulfilment point. Staff would sort whole produce and intact sealed retail packs into customer-specific orders for pickup.

Could you please advise:

1. Does this exact workflow require a Food Establishment Permit?
2. Does opening a master shipping carton while leaving each inner consumer package factory sealed alter the classification?
3. If a bulk produce case contains loose whole tomatoes, apples, broccoli, cabbage, beans, etc., may we divide those whole items among pre-ordered customer orders?
4. If loose produce is placed into a transparent customer bag, is that considered packaging or food processing for Nova Scotia purposes?
5. If weighed/bagged produce is permitted, what facility, sanitation, scale, net-quantity, and labelling requirements apply?
6. Can weekly customer pickup occur at a rented/community/commercial space, and what facility requirements would that site need to satisfy?
7. Would a recurring weekly operation be classified as a foodshop, public market, temporary event, or another establishment class?
8. If a later phase split dry shelf-stable goods from manufacturer bulk packages into smaller customer packs, what additional permit/facility requirements would apply?

The Nova Scotia Food Safety Regulations appear to exempt premises offering only fresh whole unprocessed produce and certain non-potentially-hazardous foods prepackaged at inspected facilities. I do not want to assume those exemptions cover the sorting/bagging workflow above, so I am seeking the correct classification before locking the operating model.

Thank you.

## 4. Weekly Halifax retail benchmark capture

No savings claim should rely only on the monthly Statistics Canada table.

For every candidate buy, capture at least:

- retailer;
- Halifax-area store or delivery geography;
- exact/comparable SKU;
- package quantity;
- regular price;
- promotion price if any;
- promotion limits;
- observation date/time;
- normalized unit price;
- source URL or retained evidence;
- exact/comparable/not-comparable classification.

A promotion that beats HarbourCart's model price should suppress or reprice the buy rather than being ignored.

## 5. Pilot household recruitment

The first 50-household test should ask for price-specific intent, not generic interest.

Required fields:

- product;
- household quantity;
- maximum acceptable price;
- preferred pickup day/time;
- preferred general pickup area;
- substitution tolerance;
- willingness to pre-authorize payment after a threshold is reached;
- referral source.

No payment should be collected during the initial fake-money validation round.

### Draft clarification message

**Subject:** Legal-for-trade scale requirements for pre-ordered produce buying club

Hello,

I am validating HarbourCart, a Halifax household buying club. For some potential products, a supplier may deliver a bulk case of whole produce and HarbourCart might divide it into pre-ordered household shares by weight.

Before adopting that workflow, could you please confirm the trade-measurement requirements for this fact pattern:

1. HarbourCart purchases a bulk case from a supplier.
2. Before customer pickup, HarbourCart weighs whole produce into customer-specific shares.
3. The customer is charged a fixed advertised price for a declared quantity, for example 2 kg of tomatoes or 1 kg of beans.
4. The scale is used by HarbourCart to determine the quantity placed in each customer order.

My reading of Measurement Canada's guidance is that a scale used to sell food by weight must be approved for trade, certified, and periodically inspected, with packaging/tare excluded. Please confirm whether that applies to this pre-order/prepack workflow and what type of approved scale and initial inspection/certification process would be required in Nova Scotia.

If the sale is instead by fixed numerical count, or HarbourCart transfers an intact supplier/manufacturer-sealed package without making a trade measurement, please confirm whether HarbourCart itself would avoid a trade-scale requirement for that transaction.

Thank you.
